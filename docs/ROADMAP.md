# The Long Climb — a working roadmap

The spine we walk together, one step per pull request. I bring the attempt, you
rule on it, we move to the next. This file is also the memory between sessions:
when context resets, I pick the thread back up here.

The core idea, in your words: **maximum effort into each part of an already rich
game, instead of massive overpasses that then have to be simplified.** Small,
deep, intentional. Nothing big to walk back.

## The compass

We name these to borrow the *principle* underneath, not to copy the surface.

- **Disco Elysium** — the writing carries the game. Concrete voice over cleverness.
- **Deltarune** — the world reacts to the player; characters have an interior.
- **Paper Mario: The Thousand-Year Door** — timing *inside* an attack; badges as a
  loadout you assemble.
- **The Legend of Zelda (all of it)** — a tool changes how you approach a problem,
  and every space has one real idea in it.
- **EarthBound** — the ordinary made strange; systems that carry feeling.
- **Slay the Spire** — every card and relic plugs into one coherent system. Power
  comes from synergy, not sprawl.
- **Binding of Isaac / Enter the Gungeon** — items combine into more than their
  parts. The good stuff is emergent, not scripted, and not slop.

On combat additions specifically: no mechanic that feels pasted onto a single
enemy or makes no sense inside the whole. The way to do it right is to analyze
what's already running and add with foresight, so it respects a game built with
intention. A big idea earns its size by having breadth — it shows up across the
game, not in one spot.

## The guardrails

- Mechanics live **inside the normal run**. No side modes. No bloat on the main
  screen.
- Writing stays intentional. **No tricks to prop up a sentence** — the antithesis
  ("slow is fine, wrong is what slides"), the ominous tag-clause ("...and it does
  not stay that way"), the aphoristic closer. When the trick is all you see, the
  line has failed.
- No bloat, and no obscure terms nobody knows.
- The study content — every question, every answer, every explanation — is never
  touched. It stays clinically accurate to the RBT outline.

## The backlog

### Writing audit — one surface at a time

Read each text surface closely, name what is smug, hedged, machine-made, or
bloated, rewrite it plain and concrete with the compass as reference. One surface
per pass, not one sweep.

- ✅ **Encounter blurbs** — the node descriptions. Cut the aphoristic closers, the
  antithesis tricks, the em-dash asides, and the punchy fragment closers across
  the hazards; each now states the place plainly and ties to what it does.
- ✅ **Cast & waymark stories** — Marta, Emil, the apprentice, the ptarmigan; the
  choice encounters, camp lines, and epilogues. The tales were already the
  strongest writing in the game, so this was surgical: excised the punchy
  fragment closers, the parallel-cringe ("Write true, climb true"), a couple of
  smug aphorisms, and one self-aware line ("that is the whole game"). Voice kept.
- ✅ **Examiner dialogue** — Bram, Odile, the Last Examiner, made three
  distinct voices instead of one; the enemy blurbs given a final identity pass.
- ✅ **Weather** — the four sky conditions. Cut the em-dash aside and the punchy
  "Pack patience" closer, dropped the game-y "short clocks," and made each one
  state its actual effect (faster threat, less time, less healing, or nothing).
- ○ **Oath & gear descriptions.**
- ✅ **Banners & event lines** — the mid-climb call-outs. Cut the tics (the
  em-dash asides, the fortune-cookie "all that is left is what you know") and
  built a variety system: the frequent banners (strikes, crux, knockback, last
  legs, clutch, reclaimed stones, phase, shield, streak marks) now draw from
  several clean variants at random, so the same climb stops repeating itself.
  Flavor uses `Math.random`, never the gameplay rng, so seeded runs stay
  deterministic.
- ○ **Weather, oath & gear descriptions.**
- ○ **Trailhead & UI microcopy.**

### Mechanical elevations — grown from the systems already here

No mechanic pasted onto one enemy. Every addition grows out of a system the game
already runs — threat, streaks, the Leitner tiers, the ledger and its lapses,
gear and duos, the hazard signatures — and earns its place by showing up in more
than one spot. The bigger the idea, the more of the game it should touch.

These get worked out by reading the systems closely, which the writing audit
forces anyway. So this section fills in as we go, with grounded proposals — not a
list of imported gimmicks guessed at cold.
